# Shadowraiders rules — missions, modes, and symbology

## SH-MISSION-001 — Mission attachment and tags

- **Source:** `sh-en-rulebook` — pp. 8–10, “Missions”.
- **Applies to:** Shadowraiders.
- **Maturity:** planned.
- **Rule:** A mission attaches to a pawn's mission slot during the action phase.
  Mark, cargo, and counter tags define its progress; costs are returned on
  completion or discard, and a deleted carrier discards its mission without a
  reward.

## SH-MISSION-002 — Mission rewards

- **Source:** `sh-en-rulebook` — pp. 8–10, “Mission rewards”.
- **Applies to:** Shadowraiders.
- **Maturity:** planned.
- **Rule:** A completed mission awards medals that assign control markers or
  grants control of a pawn, placing that pawn on its pawn space when required.

## SH-MODE-001 — Mode contract

- **Source:** `sh-en-rulebook` — pp. 10–15, “Game modes”.
- **Applies to:** Shadowraiders, Chaos, Outbreak, and Total War.
- **Maturity:** planned.
- **Rule:** Each mode defines player range, components, setup recipe, and
  win/lose conditions over the shared core. Shadowraiders uses missions and
  threats; Chaos and Outbreak are solo modes; Total War combines both games.

## SH-MODE-002 — Expansion-specific win and loss conditions

- **Source:** `sh-en-rulebook` — pp. 10–15, individual mode rules.
- **Applies to:** Shadowraiders modes.
- **Maturity:** planned.
- **Rule:** Shadowraiders and Total War allow control markers assigned to blocks
  and missions. Chaos can be controlled for medals, while Outbreak loses when an
  outbreak reaches Central Core 02. Exact setup and AI behavior remain
  source-backed data and engine work tracked in the backlog.

## SH-SYMBOL-001 — Added symbology

- **Source:** `sh-en-rulebook` — pp. 3–15, symbol references.
- **Applies to:** Shadowraiders.
- **Maturity:** planned.
- **Rule:** Attack dice, defense and double-ICE modifiers, direction dice,
  event spaces, pawn Black ICE, stealth, medal rewards, and Deviser control
  symbols are expansion data interpreted by their owning rule modules.
